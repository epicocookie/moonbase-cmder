# Mission 1: A Clue in the Cold

Audience: ages 7–12. Tone: curious, supportive, and safe to explore.

The outpost and its measurements are fictional. The two simulated scanner readings compare an ice-like light pattern in a shaded patch with a rock-like pattern at a nearby reference site. The report says **possible water ice**, and asks for further measurements. A reading in this game is not a new real-world discovery, and the sunlit reference does not imply the absence of water everywhere in sunlight.

## Science behind the observations

- Near the lunar poles, some crater interiors remain permanently shadowed and very cold. These conditions can preserve water ice. NASA overview: https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moon-water-and-ices/
- Scientists identified surface ice using characteristic light-absorption signatures measured by the Moon Mineralogy Mapper aboard Chandrayaan-1. NASA/JPL: https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/ice-confirmed-at-the-moons-poles/

The deployable ground scanner is an educational simplification of instrument-based observation and comparison. No physical temperature values, real geographic coordinates, or live instrument data are claimed.

The journal's Shackleton mosaic is explicitly labelled as a reference image, not a photograph of the fictional outpost. The latest completed entry is retained locally; replay replaces it only after another completed report. No names, accounts, or server are involved.

## Investigation sequence

Pip introduces an unexplained reading, then helps the player compare two samples.
At each sample, place a ground scanner. It unfolds its legs, settles onto the
marked spot, and automatically collects a four-second simulated light reading.
Completed scans enter the journal. Canceling placement keeps the device packed.
Canceling a scan after placement leaves the equipment on site for a retry.
Either site can be explored first.

After both scans, deploy the portable field screen at the marked ground station
near Communications. Its display compares the two sample visuals and illustrative
reading patterns. Confirm the cautious conclusion there before transmitting at
Communications. The equipment remains visible and survives view switches and reloads.

The transmission ends with an acknowledgement from Earth and a journal button.
Dialogue reveals gradually, with Show full text available immediately. Reduced-motion
mode shows full dialogue and static scan feedback while deployment progresses.

Sample colors, surface markings, and the screen's charts are authored visual aids.
The ground scanner and screen are fictional training equipment. They illustrate
collecting and comparing evidence without representing measured spectra or real
instrument hardware. The report continues to say possible water ice.
