Moonbase Commander is an independent educational game by the Moonbase Commander team, built to help kids fall in love with space. This page records the sources and assistance behind the current prototype.

AI / SI ASSISTANCE DISCLOSURE

Built with ChatGPT/Codex and Claude Code assistance.

Substantial portions of the code and game content were generated or revised with ChatGPT/Codex. The assistance included mission brainstorming and refinement, HTML/CSS/JavaScript, procedural 3D geometry and animation, interface design, dialogue, educational explanations, simulated scanner readings, documentation, and tests.

Mission 2, Power Through the Shadows, was implemented with Claude Code (Anthropic): its shared rule calculation, station command panels, Pip dialogue and debriefs, 2D dashboard and 3D page, menu card, tests, and documentation updates. The team specified its rules, costs, worked examples and release requirements in its own release guide; the shift rules come from the team's classic survival game.

The team supplied the goal of inspiring children through space exploration and selected the audience, expedition direction, robot companion, and journal reward. Pip uses scripted text dialogue, including the guided evidence review and transmission acknowledgement. AI-assisted procedural models include the habitat, astronaut, rover, scanning instruments, and communications dish.

The six NASA-source JPEG files listed below were downloaded from their publishers. No image-generation model or generated audio was used to create those bundled files during this development work. The existing favicon has separately noted, unverified authorship.

Science behind the story

  • NASA Science · Moon Water and Ices — references for polar shadows and the preservation of water ice.
  • NASA/JPL · Ice Confirmed at the Moon's Poles — references for identifying surface ice using instrument measurements of light.

The dialogue and explanations are AI-assisted educational adaptations of the linked references. The outpost, handheld scanner, sample readings, and mission report are fictional training content. The report describes possible water ice; no real-world discovery or real landing-site survey is claimed.

Mission 2's battery, oxygen-safety and solar numbers are simplified training units for a decision-making puzzle, not a model of real lunar power or life-support systems. It introduces no new NASA source.

NASA has not reviewed or endorsed this project or its AI-assisted content.
